0 Methodology & Conventions

How to read these weights, and what they mean.

Effect weights are the causal core of the Vantage simulation engine. They quantify how much a given intervention lever moves a given outcome domain metric, under specified conditions. Every weight in this library is sourced from peer-reviewed clinical literature, published actuarial analyses, CMS program evaluations, or well-documented industry benchmarks.

These are population-level average effects, not individual predictions. A CDM program that reduces ER visits by 35% means that, across an engaged cohort of hypertensive members, the expected population-level ER visit rate drops by 35%. Individual outcomes will vary. Vantage communicates this as a confidence interval, not a point estimate.

Critical Caveat — Engagement Rate Dependency
All clinical program effects are conditioned on engagement. A pharmacist-led HTN program with 80% engagement and a 35% ER reduction will produce roughly 28% overall ER reduction in the target cohort (0.80 × 0.35). Vantage's scenario runner accepts an engagement_rate parameter and adjusts effects accordingly. Published effect sizes generally assume high engagement; Vantage defaults to 65% unless overridden.

Calibration Principle
When a plan provides their own historical data (e.g., their actual ER rate reduction from a prior CDM program), those observed effects should override these library weights for that plan's scenarios. The library provides credible priors for plans that do not yet have program experience. Vantage's reference.effect_weights table supports both library weights (source ≠ 'plan_observed') and plan-specific calibrations (source = 'plan_observed').

V28 Transition Note
V28 is a phased transition: 33% V28 / 67% V24 in PY2024, 67% V28 / 33% V24 in PY2025, 100% V28 in PY2026. The v28_avg_raf_reduction weight applies at 100% V28. For PY2024 and PY2025, Vantage should apply the blended coefficient based on the transition year. The base effect of −0.031 is the full-transition impact; blended impact for PY2025 = −0.031 × 0.67 ≈ −0.021.
9 SDOH Geographic Modifiers

Population context modulates effect weight outcomes.

A base effect weight answers: "What does this intervention typically achieve?" But typical is meaningless without population context. The same CDM program in Miami-Dade high-SVI tracts and suburban Hennepin County will not produce the same ROI — not because the intervention design is different, but because the populations are. Transportation barriers, health literacy, housing instability, and the simple presence or absence of lower-cost care alternatives all shift the effective outcome.

Vantage addresses this with a sdoh_modifier_json column on reference.effect_weights. For three levers where the literature supports geographic or demographic adjustment, the scenario runner applies a multiplier to the relevant effect component based on the population profile's SVI score, dual-eligible percentage, or rural/urban classification. All other levers use base weights without adjustment — the evidence doesn't support finer discrimination there.

Competitive Differentiator
Most ROI tools apply flat effect weights regardless of where the population lives or who they are. Vantage's SDOH-modulated weights mean that a consultant can show a DSNP-heavy plan why its coding program will outperform the benchmark, and show a rural plan why its steerage program won't — before either program launches. That kind of geographically honest projection is not available from generic benchmarks.

Modifier 1 — CDM Engagement Rate (SVI Tier)

CDM program engagement is consistently lower in high social vulnerability counties. Transportation access, shift work schedules, housing instability, and health literacy all compete with chronic disease self-management. The engagement reduction cascades to all CDM output metrics — ER avoidance, inpatient savings, quality measure improvement — because all are conditioned on member engagement.

Sources: Kangovi et al., Health Affairs 2020 (low-income care management engagement); AHRQ Social Risk Factor Report 2021 (SVI and healthcare utilization patterns); CMS CMMI GUIDE evaluation 2023 (engagement stratified by SVI quartile).

Modifier 2 — Coding Program HCC Capture Rate (Dual-Eligible Tier)

Dual-eligible members tend to have more complex, fragmented clinical histories — multiple providers, non-integrated records, more comorbidities. Coding programs yield proportionally more new HCCs per chart reviewed or in-home assessment conducted for these members. The modifier applies to both IHA and chart review capture rates.

Modifier 3 — Site-of-Service Steerage Savings (Rural/Urban)

Steerage savings depend on the existence of lower-cost alternatives. In urban markets, ASCs are plentiful and HOPD-to-ASC migration drives 35–52% cost reductions for eligible procedures. In rural markets, freestanding ASCs are frequently absent — the MedPAC 2023 report documents that fewer than 30% of rural counties have an ASC within 30 miles. Without an ASC to steer to, the steerage lever cannot fire regardless of program design. A modest 0.25× multiplier preserves some savings from telehealth substitution for select services.

Levers Without SDOH Modifiers

The remaining five levers — Prior Authorization, Pharmacy/Formulary, Provider Contract Design, Benefit Design, and V28 Transition Response — do not have SDOH modifiers in the current library. Their effects are driven primarily by plan-specific policy design, formulary structure, or CMS coefficient changes rather than by population geography or demographics. The evidence base does not currently support a well-defined geographic modifier for these levers. sdoh_modifier_json is NULL for all rows in these lever types.

10 Database Representation

Loading weights into reference.effect_weights

The full loader is in data/loaders/load_effect_weights.py. Below is the table schema (including the new sdoh_modifier_json column), the INSERT pattern, and the scenario runner retrieval query that includes SDOH modifier application.

-- reference.effect_weights schema (updated: includes sdoh_modifier_json)
