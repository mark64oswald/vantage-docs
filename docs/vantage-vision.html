1 The Problem Space

Healthcare strategy is made in silos. The problem is the gaps between them.

Medicare Advantage plans, ACOs, and risk-bearing provider organizations operate across three interconnected financial domains — risk adjustment, quality performance, and medical cost management. Each domain drives a distinct P&L line. Each is served by a different set of vendors, analysts, and organizational teams. And each is almost never analyzed in relationship to the others.

The result is a systematic blind spot in strategic decision-making. When a plan asks "Should we invest in a diabetes management program?", the risk adjustment team models the RAF impact, the quality team models the Stars impact, and the finance team models the TCOC impact — in separate tools, in separate meetings, over several weeks. By the time a synthesized answer arrives at the C-suite, the business context may have already shifted.

📊

Siloed Analytics

Risk adjustment, quality, and cost management operate in separate vendor ecosystems. No existing product integrates all three for strategic simulation.

⏱

Weeks, Not Minutes

Cross-domain analysis requires coordinating actuaries, quality analysts, and finance teams. "What's the integrated ROI of Program X?" can take weeks to answer.

🔮

No Simulation Capability

Incumbent vendors (Cotiviti, Inovalon, Optum) excel at measuring what happened. None provide self-service strategic simulation of what could happen.

💰

High Stakes, No Safety Net

A wrong bet on a $3M care management program can mean a 3-star rating, a lost quality bonus, and $50M+ in downstream revenue impact. The stakes demand better tools.

The Medicare Advantage Opportunity Is Perfectly Aligned

Medicare Advantage represents the purest expression of value-based care — the only payment model where the three domains map directly, cleanly, and independently to three separate revenue and margin line items:

Domain 01

Risk Adjustment

CMS-HCC model scores each member's condition burden. Risk scores determine base capitation revenue. The V24→V28 transition is creating immediate urgency across the industry.

P&L Line → Base Capitation Revenue
Domain 02

Stars / Quality

~40 HEDIS/CAHPS measures produce a 1–5 star rating. A 4-star plan earns a 5% quality bonus plus enhanced rebates. Cut-point movement is a competitive intelligence problem.

P&L Line → Quality Bonus Revenue
Domain 03

Medical Cost / MLR

Total Cost of Care vs. capitation revenue determines medical margin. Utilization management, prior auth, site-of-service steerage, and care management are the levers.

P&L Line → Medical Margin
The Market Gap
The vendor ecosystem mirrors the organizational silos: risk adjustment vendors optimize RAF, quality vendors optimize Stars, care management platforms optimize utilization. Nobody sells integrated strategic simulation across all three. That's the Vantage thesis — and it's genuinely differentiated.

Prior Authorization: A Fourth Lever Hiding in Plain Sight

Prior authorization has emerged as arguably the #1 pain point in Medicare Advantage, with health systems dropping MA contracts over PA denial rates, active CMS pilot programs, and pending legislation. But PA isn't simply another measurement domain — it's an operational control lever whose effects ripple through all three domains simultaneously.

When a PA policy changes — whether tightened or relaxed, or dynamically modulated by VBC contract status — the downstream effects are traceable and simulatable: PA decisions influence whether conditions get diagnosed and documented (risk adjustment impact), whether preventive or management services get delivered (quality measure impact), and whether high-cost acute episodes are avoided or triggered (medical cost impact).

This makes PA a natural simulation variable in Vantage: not a peer domain, but a lever the platform exposes — with the three measurement domains as the dependent outcome variables that respond when you turn it. The FHIR Da Vinci implementation guides (Coverage Requirements Discovery, Prior Authorization Support, CDex) provide the emerging interoperability infrastructure that makes this tractable for real-world data integration.

2 Solution Requirements

Healthcare Value Intelligence: One Surface. One Conversation.

Vantage is a strategic simulation engine built as a conversational AI platform. Users interact through natural language. The platform models how proposed interventions — clinical programs, operational changes, policy adjustments, prior auth configurations — would impact risk adjustment revenue, Stars ratings, and medical cost simultaneously, before a dollar is committed.

Core Design Principle
Vantage is not an operational tool. It doesn't send outreach, manage workflows, or replace vendors like Cotiviti or Optum. It is an intelligence layer — a strategic simulation surface that sits on top of existing data and finds the cross-domain opportunities that siloed tools miss.

Functional Requirements

Population Engine

Generate or ingest a synthetic population profile matching a target plan's demographics, geography, and condition mix. This is the prerequisite for all simulation — you need a population to model interventions against. The engine should accept calibration parameters (member count, geographic market, condition prevalence targets) and produce a member set with realistic HCC profiles, quality measure baselines, and utilization patterns.

Risk Adjustment Model

Implement the CMS-HCC model in both V24 and V28 variants. For any population, calculate per-member RAF scores, identify suspect conditions (coded vs. probable uncoded), and model the revenue impact of coding improvement programs. The V24/V28 dual-scoring capability is the immediate market entry point — every MA plan needs to understand their transition exposure.

Quality / Stars Model

Evaluate eCQM/HEDIS measures (APP Plus priority set) against the population. Identify gaps. Project measure performance under proposed interventions. Model Stars rating implications using the CMS clustering methodology — cut-points are relative, not fixed, making this a competitive intelligence problem. Identify which measures have the most leverage on the overall rating and where the cliff edges are.

Medical Margin / VBC Model

Calculate TCOC against risk-adjusted benchmarks. Model the cost impact of utilization management interventions, care management programs, and site-of-service changes. Produce MLR projections. This is the most complex model (stochastic rather than deterministic) but the most financially consequential for strategic decisions.

Intervention Lever Framework

Define a typed intervention library: clinical programs (chronic disease management, pharmacy MTM, in-home assessments), operational controls (prior auth policies, formulary changes, network steerage), and regulatory responses (V28 transition strategies). Each lever has typed parameters (enrollment rate, compliance target, effect size), and the framework routes lever activations through all three domain models simultaneously to produce cross-domain impact projections.

Scenario Runner & Comparator

Run a proposed intervention against the population and produce risk + quality + cost impact in one view. Compare multiple scenarios side-by-side. Normalize for investment level. Produce confidence intervals rather than point estimates. Support multi-year projections with member churn, condition progression, and compounding effects.

Output Layer

Generate presentation-ready executive summaries (PDF/PPTX), interactive HTML dashboards, and scenario archives. A "Triple-Play" summary card for each scenario — the single most important output format — shows the intervention design and its projected impact across all three domains in a format suitable for board-level investment decisions.

Non-Functional Requirements

The platform must be operable by healthcare strategists and consultants without data engineering skills. Responses should arrive in conversational time (seconds, not minutes). All outputs must include transparency about assumptions and confidence levels — healthcare decision-makers need to understand the model's reasoning, not just its conclusions. PHI safety is non-negotiable; the system operates on synthetic or de-identified data.

4 Intervention Levers & Outcome Domains

Levers are what you dial. Domains are what you measure.

The core insight that makes Vantage's architecture coherent is the distinction between intervention levers and outcome domains. Levers are operational inputs — things a plan or provider organization can decide to do or change. Domains are the measurement systems that capture the financial consequences of those decisions. The simulation engine connects levers to domains through evidence-based effect models.

The Prior Authorization Lever in Depth

Prior authorization deserves special attention because of its unique position in the framework. Unlike clinical programs (which primarily affect cost with secondary risk/quality effects), PA operates as a gating mechanism on the entire care delivery system. Its simulation parameters include:

PA Simulation Parameters
Service coverage (which CPT/HCPCS codes require PA) · Approval threshold (approval rate target for in-network, out-of-network) · VBC modulation (dynamic threshold adjustment based on contract performance) · Denial policy (criteria type, peer-to-peer availability) · Turnaround time (standard vs. expedited) · Appeal configuration. Each parameter produces downstream effects traceable through all three outcome domains.

FHIR Da Vinci Integration Path

The HL7 Da Vinci project has produced a suite of implementation guides that define the interoperability infrastructure for the PA domain: Coverage Requirements Discovery (CRD) surfaces coverage rules in the clinical workflow; Documentation Templates and Coverage Rules (DTR) automates documentation gathering; Prior Authorization Support (PAS) standardizes the submission and response workflow; and Clinical Data Exchange (CDex) enables payer-initiated data requests from providers.

For Vantage, the immediate value is not live FHIR integration but structural alignment: designing the internal data model (pa_events, lever_configs) to mirror the semantic structure of these IGs means that future ingestion of real FHIR PA data becomes a mapping exercise rather than a schema redesign. It also creates a credibility signal with technically sophisticated customers who understand the Da Vinci ecosystem.

5 Data Model

Four schemas, cleanly separated, designed for simulation.

The Vantage data model uses DuckDB for local development and early deployment, with a DataStore abstraction layer designed for future migration to Databricks or Snowflake. Four schema namespaces organize the data: reference data (stable CMS/NCQA specifications), population data (the simulation subjects), scenario data (the simulation configurations), and output data (the simulation results).

── SCHEMA: reference ────────────────────────────────────────────────
-- CMS-HCC crosswalk and coefficient tables
CREATE TABLE reference.hcc_crosswalk (
  icd10_code      VARCHAR,
  cc_number       INTEGER,
  hcc_number      INTEGER,  -- NULL if CC but not payment-relevant HCC
  model_year      SMALLINT,   -- 24 | 28
  effective_date  DATE
);

CREATE TABLE reference.raf_coefficients (
  hcc_number      INTEGER,
  model_year      SMALLINT,
  segment         VARCHAR,   -- CNA | CND | CFA | CFD | CPA | CPD | INS | NE
  coefficient     DECIMAL(8,4)
);

CREATE TABLE reference.measure_specs (
  measure_id      VARCHAR,    -- e.g. 'CMS165v12'
  measure_name    VARCHAR,
  steward         VARCHAR,    -- NCQA | CMS | AMA
  canonical_url   VARCHAR,    -- FHIR canonical for CQL library
  stars_domain    VARCHAR,    -- Clinical | Experience | Access | Part D
  stars_weight    DECIMAL(4,2),
  effective_year  SMALLINT
);

CREATE TABLE reference.pa_criteria_templates (
  service_code    VARCHAR,    -- CPT / HCPCS
  service_desc    VARCHAR,
  pa_required     BOOLEAN,
  criteria_type   VARCHAR,    -- MCG | InterQual | custom
  vbc_modifiable  BOOLEAN     -- can VBC status modulate this requirement?
);
── SCHEMA: population ───────────────────────────────────────────────
CREATE TABLE population.members (
  member_id         VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,   -- synthetic, never real PHI
  birth_year        SMALLINT,
  sex               CHAR(1),
  dual_status       BOOLEAN,
  orec              SMALLINT,    -- original reason for entitlement code
  county_fips       VARCHAR,
  attribution_pcp   VARCHAR,     -- NPI of attributed PCP
  enrollment_start  DATE,
  enrollment_end    DATE          -- NULL = currently enrolled
);

CREATE TABLE population.member_hccs (
  member_id    VARCHAR,
  hcc_number   INTEGER,
  model_year   SMALLINT,
  source       VARCHAR,  -- claims | chart_review | iha | suspect | npl
  confidence   DECIMAL(4,3)  -- 1.0 = confirmed; <1.0 = suspect
);

CREATE TABLE population.pa_events (
  pa_event_id      VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,
  member_id        VARCHAR,
  service_code     VARCHAR,    -- CPT/HCPCS
  diagnosis_codes  VARCHAR[],  -- ICD-10 codes supporting the request
  request_date     DATE,
  disposition      VARCHAR,    -- approved | denied | partial | withdrawn
  auth_period      DATERANGE,
  denial_reason    VARCHAR,
  da_vinci_ref     VARCHAR     -- FHIR Claim.id from PAS IG (future)
);
── SCHEMA: scenario ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
CREATE TABLE scenario.scenarios (
  scenario_id    VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,
  name           VARCHAR,
  description    VARCHAR,
  population_id  VARCHAR,   -- which population snapshot
  created_at     TIMESTAMP,
  status         VARCHAR    -- draft | running | complete
);

CREATE TABLE scenario.lever_configs (
  lever_id       VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,
  scenario_id    VARCHAR,
  lever_type     VARCHAR,   -- coding_program | cdm | pa_policy | formulary | steerage | v28_response
  parameters     JSON,      -- typed by lever_type; see lever spec per type
  target_cohort  VARCHAR   -- SQL predicate or cohort_id
);

-- Example parameters for lever_type = 'pa_policy':
-- { "services": ["CPT99213","CPT76805"], "approval_rate": 0.92,
--   "vbc_modulation": true, "vbc_threshold_pct": 0.95,
--   "turnaround_hours": 72, "peer_to_peer": true }

CREATE TABLE scenario.contract_configs (
  contract_id      VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,
  scenario_id      VARCHAR,
  contract_type    VARCHAR,   -- mssp | reach | ma_vbc | cpci
  benchmark_pct    DECIMAL(5,3),
  savings_share    DECIMAL(4,3),
  quality_gates    JSON       -- conditions on quality measure thresholds
);
── SCHEMA: output ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
CREATE TABLE output.member_raf (
  scenario_id   VARCHAR,
  member_id     VARCHAR,
  model_year    SMALLINT,  -- 24 | 28
  raf_baseline  DECIMAL(8,4),
  raf_projected DECIMAL(8,4),
  delta_revenue DECIMAL(10,2)
);

CREATE TABLE output.measure_gaps (
  scenario_id          VARCHAR,
  member_id            VARCHAR,
  measure_id           VARCHAR,
  population_type      VARCHAR,  -- IPP | denominator | numerator | exclusion
  gap_status           BOOLEAN,  -- true = gap open (not in numerator)
  closure_probability  DECIMAL(4,3),
  reachability         VARCHAR   -- reachable | excluded | lost_to_followup
);

CREATE TABLE output.member_radv_risk (
  scenario_id              VARCHAR,
  member_id                VARCHAR,
  hcc_id                   INTEGER,
  documentation_complete   BOOLEAN,
  documentation_specificit VARCHAR,  -- high | medium | low
  encounter_to_doc_lag     INTEGER,  -- days between encounter and HCC support
  audit_risk_score         DECIMAL(4,3)
);
DuckDB Lessons from HealthSim
The data layer applies hard-won lessons from HealthSim's concurrency challenges: use the close-before-write pattern (always close read connections before opening write connections), implement retry logic with exponential backoff for lock contention, eliminate Git LFS by keeping DuckDB files small through schema partitioning, and maintain the DataStore abstraction so the entire data layer can be swapped for Databricks or Snowflake without changing skill logic.

  • Slower revenue ramp; requires community investment
  • Competitors can fork and productize
  • More complex commercial model to execute
Commercial SaaS

Direct SaaS to Mid-Market Plans

Sell Vantage directly to mid-size MA plans (30K–300K members) and provider-sponsored plans who face the same strategic decisions as large plans but lack sophisticated in-house analytics.

  • Clean ARR model; directly captures product value
  • Customer relationships drive product roadmap
  • Mid-market segment underserved by incumbents
  • Long sales cycles (6–12 months); relationship-heavy
  • Requires reference customers before broad traction
  • Procurement risk without proven track record
Consulting Channel

Healthcare Consulting Firm Channel

Target healthcare consulting firms (Guidehouse, Milliman, Oliver Wyman, boutiques) as the initial channel. Consultants leverage Vantage across multiple client engagements, while plans build internal capabilities.

  • Fastest path to market — consultants buy and use tools fast
  • Reference customers without direct plan procurement risk
  • Revenue per engagement model is natural fit
  • Consulting firms have existing plan relationships
  • Consultants may not convert to plan-direct revenue
  • Margin sharing reduces long-term value capture
  • Product roadmap driven by consulting use cases, not plan needs
Recommended Sequencing
The most defensible path: consulting channel first (prove the tool works and generates value in real engagements), OSS core in parallel (build community and credibility, especially around Da Vinci/FHIR alignment), then direct SaaS to mid-market plans once reference customers and case studies exist. This sequences risk appropriately and avoids the chicken-and-egg problem of selling to plans without a track record.
